3 · Channel balance (gain + noise-floor calibration)

  1. Select Both in section 1.
  2. Put a steady sound source centred in front, equally far from both mics (about 30 cm): talk continuously, or play music / noise.
  3. Press Calibrate. Stay silent until the page says MAKE SOUND, then keep the sound steady until it finishes.
Idle.

Gain / boost (also called trim) is extra gain added to that one channel, on top of the firmware's digital gain in section 1. Positive boosts the channel, negative cuts it, 0 leaves it untouched. The calibration sets it for you; use these boxes to fine-tune by hand.

Active settings: —

    Two things are balanced so the meters agree both during sound and at rest. The gain (boost) on the weaker mic matches the signal level. Because that also boosts its noise, a gate then pulls the boosted channel's noise back to the other channel's noise floor whenever nothing is playing; it opens when the mic's own level rises clearly above its own noise. The tables show what the meters read in both situations, measured by running the captured audio through the same processing the live path uses. Neither step improves that mic's signal-to-noise ratio: a very weak mic's output is mostly noise, and the page warns when that is the case. Calibrate again after re-wiring or replacing a mic. Settings are saved and re-applied on restart; the meters, direction, recordings and STT use the processed audio, while the raw I2S readout in section 1 stays untouched. The raw audio of the last calibration run is kept in calibration_last_run.wav.

    7 · Wiring diagnostic (raw I2S bits)

    Looks at the microphones' raw 32-bit data words, before any gain or filtering, to tell a word-select (WS) / clock (SCK) / data (SD) problem from an ordinary noisy or weak microphone. Capture in a quiet room. A healthy INMP441 sends 24 data bits at the top of each 32-bit word and leaves the lowest bits at 0; lost sync shows up as full-scale-looking garbage or as bits shifted into those idle low bits. It also looks for a glitch that repeats every I2S buffer, which points at electrical coupling from the ESP32 itself.

    Idle.

      Wi-Fi test: take one normal capture and one with Wi-Fi audio paused. If the repeating glitch is there normally and gone when Wi-Fi is paused, the ESP32's Wi-Fi transmissions are coupling into that microphone (power or ground). These are fingerprints, not proof. They say what the bits are consistent with; the physical checks (continuity of the WS wire, resolder, swap the wire sets) settle it. Needs the mic_lab firmware version 2 or newer. The raw capture is kept in diag_last_capture.npy.
